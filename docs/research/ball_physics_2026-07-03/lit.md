# Table-tennis ball physics, low-speed regime — web literature findings

Ball constants used throughout: D=0.04 m, R=0.02 m, m=2.7 g, A=1.2566e-3 m², air ρ≈1.204 kg/m³, ν=1.5e-5 m²/s. Conversion for v0's form (a = −k_d|v|v + k_m(ω×v)): **k_d = ρ·A·C_D/(2m) = 0.280·C_D [1/m]**.

## 1) Drag C_d vs Re

FACTS
- Re = vD/ν = 2667·v: **v=2 → Re 5.3e3; v=5 → 1.33e4; v=8 → 2.13e4; v=15 → 4.0e4; v=25 → 6.67e4**. All subcritical (smooth-sphere drag crisis Re≈2–3.5e5 ⇒ v≈75–130 m/s, unreachable). Miyazaki's flight experiments confirm the whole game lives at Re < 1e5 ([phys.org on Miyazaki EJP 2017](https://phys.org/news/2017-06-fluid-mechanics-table-tennis-ballsdiscovery.html); [ADS: EJPh 38, 024001](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017EJPh...38b4001M/abstract), flight-measured range 3.0e4<Re<9.0e4, 0<SP<1).
- LES-CFD of a spinning 40 mm ball, **exactly our regime** (V=2.5–20 m/s, 15–90 rps, Re 6.6e3–5.3e4, SP 0.094–4.524; Ito & Ueshima, Trans. JSST 17(1):25, [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tjsst/17/1/17_25/_article/-char/en); values from PDF Table 2): C_D at 15 rps: 0.583 (2.5 m/s), 0.498 (5), 0.475 (10), 0.490 (15–20). At 90 rps: 0.588 (2.5), 0.588 (5), 0.510 (10), 0.548–0.555 (15–20). **Span 0.475–0.620**; C_D rises ~10–25% at the lowest speed and rises with spin at fixed speed. Authors report qualitative agreement with wind-tunnel and flight experiments.
- Equivalent k_d: C_D 0.475→k_d 0.133; C_D 0.5→0.140; C_D 0.62→0.174 [1/m]. So k_d is constant only to **±15%** across 2.5–20 m/s and 15–90 rps.
- Simple treatments use C_d=0.5 flat ([U. Physics Competition team paper](http://www.uphysicsc.com/2012-GM-B-414.PDF)); IntechOpen "Table Tennis and Physics" gives C_D and C_L only as curves vs u/v ([IntechOpen ch. 83844](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/83844)).

JUDGMENT: within 2–8 m/s alone (Re 5e3–2e1e4), C_D is *not quite* constant — the CFD grid shows ~0.5→0.6 drift toward 2.5 m/s and a spin-coupled increase; a single k_d fit on 2–8 m/s data will be ~10–20% high if reused at 15–25 m/s. That is precisely Sony's reported failure mode (below).

## 2) Magnus / lift vs spin ratio; saturation & inverse Magnus

FACTS
- SR = ωR/v examples: **30 rev/s @ 3 m/s → SR = 188.5·0.02/3 = 1.26**; @2 m/s → 1.88; @8 m/s → 0.47; 60 rev/s @ 3 m/s → 2.51. Pro (25 m/s, 120 rev/s) → 0.60. Low-speed play sits at SR≈0.5–2.5, *higher* than pro play.
- Same CFD paper, Table 3 (C_L, force normalized by v²): at 2.5 m/s, C_L = 0.373 (SP 0.75) → 0.418 (SP 4.52): **C_L saturates at ≈0.37–0.42 while SP grows 6×**. At 5 m/s: 0.27–0.39. At 10–20 m/s and low SP, C_L = 0.07–0.32, roughly increasing with SP (pre-saturation branch). Table 4 gives the "Magnus coefficient" C_M used for the trajectory equation of motion (ω×v normalization, i.e. the k_m-like quantity): at 2.5 m/s it **falls 0.185 → 0.035 (5.3×) as spin goes 15→90 rps**, and at fixed 15 rps it rises 0.185→0.338 from 2.5→15 m/s. Both tables say the same thing: Magnus force does not scale as ω·v once SP ≳ 0.5–1; an effective k_m is a strong function of SR (and weakly of Re).
- Table 5 (fluid torque coefficient Cm): 1.1e-2 (2.5 m/s, 15 rps) up to 2.2e-1 (2.5 m/s, 90 rps) — spin decay is largest exactly at low speed + high spin; v0's flight model keeps ω constant.
- **Lift crisis / "spin crisis"** (Miyazaki et al., EJP 38, 024001, 2017): C_L is non-monotonic in SP with a deep valley near **SP≈0.5, and lift nearly vanishes at Re=9.0e4 (≈34 m/s) for 0.48<SP<0.5** ([ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017EJPh...38b4001M/abstract), [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2017-06-fluid-mechanics-table-tennis-ballsdiscovery.html)). This is a high-Re phenomenon — at Re 5e3–2e4 (our 2–8 m/s) the CFD grid shows **no negative C_L anywhere** (all Table-3 entries positive, 0.073–0.418).
- Inverse (negative) Magnus on smooth spheres is a critical-regime effect (Re around 2e5+; [Kim et al. 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261028356_Negative_Magnus_lift_on_a_rotating_sphere_at_around_the_critical_Reynolds_number); Muto et al., cited in the CFD paper's refs) — not reachable by a TT ball. IntechOpen notes reported "inverse Magnus at low spins" causes only millimeter-level deviations in TT ([IntechOpen](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/83844), Sec. 3.1).

JUDGMENT: for 2–8 m/s play the danger is not inverse Magnus (absent in this Re band) but **saturation**: with constant k_m fit at SR≈0.2–0.5 (fast/low-spin balls), extrapolating to SR≈1.5–2.5 (slow loopy balls) overpredicts Magnus force ~2–4× (implied C_L∝SR would reach 1.0–1.5 vs measured plateau ≈0.4). Conversely, k_m fit on the team's low-speed/high-SR mocap data will *underpredict* curvature of faster balls by a similar factor. A C_L(SP) = a·SP/(b+SP)-type law or lookup is the literature-consistent fix.

## 3) Table bounce

FACTS
- ITTF spec: 24–26 cm rebound from 30.5 cm drop onto steel ⇒ **e = 0.887–0.923 at v_n = 2.45 m/s** ([Wikipedia COR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_restitution); echoed in [arXiv 2604.11349](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11349)).
- Rémond/Rinaldi/Géminard (PRE 106, 2022; PRE 107, 2023; conference abstract [sportsphysics24](https://sportsphysics24.sciencesconf.org/585410/document)): normal impact on rigid surface — **shell buckles when crushing exceeds ~2× shell thickness; above buckling onset COR drops**, extra loss mainly friction between buckled shell and substrate ([RG record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361954850_Dynamical_buckling_of_a_table-tennis_ball_impinging_normally_on_a_rigid_target_Experimental_and_numerical_studies)). IntechOpen quantifies: buckling for impact speeds **above ~5 m/s**, COR falling "from 0.9 to 0.8 or even below"; contact time ≈0.7 ms ([IntechOpen](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/83844) Sec. 2). Below buckling (v_n = 1–4 m/s, i.e., table bounces of 2–8 m/s play), the orthogonal COR "depends only slightly on incident velocities" (survey language in [arXiv 2604.11349](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11349)).
- Oblique bounce, no spin (Rémond PRE 107, [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-04119819v1/document) — full text behind bot-block): **below a critical incidence angle the ball leaves the surface rolling without sliding**; above it, sliding persists through contact. This is the standard Garwin/Cross two-regime picture (tangential COR framework: [Cross, AJP horizontal COR](https://physics.umd.edu/courses/Phys405/Hill/Fall05/Information/AJP/AJP00482.pdf); [Cross, tangential compliance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228926119_Impact_of_a_ball_on_a_surface_with_tangential_compliance)). Plastic balls: friction coefficient higher at lower contact-point tangential speed (IntechOpen Sec. 2) — i.e., μ itself is state-dependent.
- Robot-TT practice: the standard table rebound model (TRM) is **Nonomura, Nakashima & Hayakawa, SICE 2010**, "Analysis of effects of rebounds and aerodynamics for trajectory of table tennis ball" ([RG](https://researchgate.net/publication/224182830_Analysis_of_effects_of_rebounds_and_aerodynamics_for_trajectory_of_table_tennis_ball)) — still used verbatim in 2025 spin-aware MPC ([arXiv 2510.08754](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08754), Sec. III-B cites "table rebound model from Nonomura et al."). The MPI gray-box paper instead fits a **full unconstrained 6×6 linear map C: (v⁺,ω⁺)=C(v⁻,ω⁻)** from data ([arXiv 2305.15189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15189), Eq. 2) — i.e., they abandoned hand-parameterized restitution entirely.
- **cos(θ) tangential-gain family (v0's s(θ)=(a_t+b_t·cosθ)|u_t|, spin_contact.py:13):** I could NOT locate this exact form in the open literature (searched bounce-model + incident-angle + tangential-coefficient combinations). Nearest relatives are impact-state-dependent tangential-impulse models (Nakashima 2010 rubber model; Cross's angle-dependent grip-slip transition; 2604.11349's k_p(v_s,v_z)). JUDGMENT: it is most plausibly transcribed from the Sony Ace supplementary methods (v0 was "mocap-calibrated … Ace-style"); treat it as an empirical fit form validated only in whatever regime its source data covered — verify against the local Ace PDF, not the web.

## 4) Racket rubber contact

FACTS
- **Best modern dataset** — "Learning Racket-Ball Bounce Dynamics Across Diverse Rubbers" ([arXiv 2604.11349](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11349), Apr 2026): 8,194 bounce events, 10 rubbers, launcher limited to **≤12 m/s, ≤125 rps** (explicitly flagged as below professional level). Baseline impulse model v⁺=Av⁻+Bω⁻, ω⁺=Cv⁻+Dω⁻. Values: inverted offensive rubbers COR **0.65–0.75 at low speed**; allround 0.60–0.68; anti-spin 0.53 (μ=0.197); tangential impulse gain k_p≈0.019 for inverted, far lower for pips; long pips highly nonlinear. Crucially: **"COR … decreases approximately linearly with increasing |v_z| … differences of up to 0.15 between low (~2 m/s) and high (~12 m/s) velocities"**, slopes ≈0.021 (offensive) / 0.017 (allround) per m/s; k_p depends on both v_s and v_z. GP regression beats the constant-parameter analytic model on all 10 rubbers.
- Rate dependence of the polymer stack: Rinaldi et al., "Non Linearity of the Ball/Rubber Impact in Table Tennis," Procedia Eng. 2016 ([HAL](https://hal.science/hal-01802288v1), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877705816307548)): **restitution coefficients decrease with increasing launch velocity; dynamic modulus and yield strength exceed quasi-static values**; friction + rate-dependent viscoelasticity dominate dissipation; minimum COR when rubber+sponge are assembled.
- Canonical spin-transfer model: Nakashima et al. 2010, "Modeling of rebound phenomenon between ball and racket rubber with spinning effect" ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229033631_Modeling_of_rebound_phenomenon_between_ball_and_racket_rubber_with_spinning_effect)) — tangential KE stored elastically in rubber, tangential impulse ∝ contact-point velocity (allows spin reversal). Soft coatings mainly increase spin, barely change normal restitution (Rémond/Rinaldi abstract, [sportsphysics24](https://sportsphysics24.sciencesconf.org/585410/document)).
- DeepMind found ball–rubber restitution **bimodal in incoming spin**: paddle modeled as spring-damper on two passive joints with damping ≈0 for topspin contacts vs ≈10³ for underspin, solver params switched at runtime by pre-contact spin sign ([arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)).

## 5) Sony Ace replication/critique + other systems' ball models

FACTS
- No peer-reviewed replication of Ace's ball model exists yet (paper published 2026-04-23: [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10338-5); [Sony AI project page](https://ace.ai.sony/)). Secondary reporting of its development history states the sim ball model "worked well for slower shots" but **overestimated drag for high-velocity strikes (~19.6 m/s, 450 rad/s ≈ 72 rev/s), producing policies that overshot the real table; the fix was recalibrating drag coefficients for the high-speed regime, not the algorithm** ([Medium/creedtec analysis](https://medium.com/@creed_1732/sony-project-aces-sim-to-real-robot-training-the-physics-model-failure-that-nearly-derailed-the-cba441580620)). Non-technical critique focuses on generalization/replication cost, not the physics ([implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/sonys-ace-robot-beat-elite-table-tennis-players-the-harder-match-starts-now/)). Note this is exactly the mirror-image of the HOPE risk: they fit low-speed-first and broke at high speed.
- **DeepMind (2408.03906)**: MuJoCo ellipsoid stateless fluid model; blunt-drag coefficient *measured*, slender-drag default, angular drag zero, **Kutta and Magnus lift left at MuJoCo defaults**; ball-table and ball-rubber contact solver params from empirical sweeps optimizing sim-to-real ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)). Amateur-speed system.
- **MPI gray-box (2305.15189)**: same ODE family as v0 (v̇ = −k_d‖v‖v + k_m(ω×v) + g, their Eq. 1) but k_d, k_m are **per-trajectory EKF states (k=a²+ε, ε=0.05) learned online, not global constants**, plus data-fit 6×6 impact map; launcher data at amateur speeds, 180 Hz cameras. Their design choice is itself evidence that global constants underfit.
- **Spin-aware quadrupedal MPC ([arXiv 2510.08754](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08754))**: identical lumped model (Eq. 1, constants C_D, C_M characterized with a Spinsight system) + Nonomura TRM; **model-based spin estimation with those constants achieved only R²=0.42; a learned residual network was needed to reach R²=0.70** — direct quantitative evidence of unmodeled state-dependence in the constant-coefficient form.
- **Omron Forpheus**: proprietary prediction model; spin inferred from trajectory curvature/deceleration with standard 80 fps cameras; no published coefficient values ([Omron Technics vol. 51](https://www.omron.com/global/en/technology/omrontechnics/vol51/016.html)).

## JUDGMENT — three biggest quantitative risks in constant k_d, k_m, e_eff for 2–8 m/s

1. **Constant k_m vs Magnus saturation (largest risk, ~2–4× force error).** Low-speed play spans SR 0.5–2.5, squarely in the saturation region (C_L plateaus ≈0.4 by SP≈1; effective ω×v-coefficient falls ~5× from SP 0.75→4.5 in the Ito/Ueshima tables). A k_m fit on the venue's low-speed/high-SR strikes will underpredict curvature of faster low-SR balls by ~2×+, and any k_m fit at moderate SR overpredicts lift on slow spinny balls by 2–4×. This aliases directly into landing-point reward: at 3 m/s a factor-2 Magnus error changes drop distance by tens of cm. Fix: k_m→k_m(SR) (saturating law or 2-D lookup in Re×SP); also add spin decay (torque coefficient is maximal exactly at low v + high ω, Table 5).
2. **Constant e_eff on the racket vs measured rate dependence (~0.02/m·s⁻¹).** Racket COR drops ≈0.017–0.021 per m/s of normal impact speed (Δ≈0.15 over 2→12 m/s, arXiv 2604.11349; independent confirmation Rinaldi 2016), and tangential gain k_p depends on both v_s and v_z (strongly for pips). e_eff calibrated on low-speed venue strikes will overestimate outgoing speed at match-pace contacts by ~5–10%, and DeepMind's topspin/underspin bimodality warns that a single tangential gain across spin signs is already wrong. Table-side is safer at 1–4 m/s normal speeds, but ball buckling above ~5 m/s (COR 0.9→0.8) will bite as play speeds rise.
3. **Constant k_d across regimes (±15–20%).** C_D drifts 0.475→0.62 with speed and spin inside 2.5–20 m/s (k_d 0.133–0.174), with the low-speed end biased high — so k_d fit at 2–8 m/s transplanted to 15–25 m/s repeats Sony's documented failure (drag overestimated at high speed → simulated returns overshoot the real table; they had to re-fit drag at 19.6 m/s before RL transferred). Also note C_D's spin dependence means drag and Magnus calibration are coupled — fit them jointly per (Re, SP) cell, not sequentially.

Secondary caveat: the a_t + b_t·cosθ tangential contact form has no located open-literature pedigree; assume it inherits only the validation regime of its (likely Ace-supplement) source, and re-validate the sliding/rolling transition angle against the venue's racket-tagged data (Rémond PRE 107 shows a sharp roll/slide regime change with incidence angle even without spin).
