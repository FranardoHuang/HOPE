# Adversarial verification — low-speed regime quantitative claims

Primary evidence I obtained: full text of the Ito CFD paper (extracted to `/tmp/claude-0/-workspace-yikang/56ec1c84-c2df-4667-8842-59b9f726e183/scratchpad/ito.txt`), the ISJOS COR paper (`.../isjos.txt`), and the IOP drop-test paper (`.../drop.txt`), plus abstracts of Miyazaki EJP 2017 and Rémond PRE 2022.

## Sub-claim 1: Re range 2–8 m/s and Cd "effectively constant" — CONFIRMED with a quantified caveat

FACTS
- Arithmetic: Re = vD/ν (D=0.04, ν=1.5e-5) = 2667·v → **5.33e3 (2 m/s) to 2.13e4 (8 m/s)**; recomputed independently, matches both agents. Drag crisis (~2–3.5e5) unreachable. ✓
- Non-spinning smooth sphere, Newton regime (1e3 ≤ Re < 3e5): Cd nearly constant, **min 0.38 at Re=5e3, max 0.50 at Re=7e4, mean ≈0.44–0.46** ([settling-particles review, arXiv 2009.02250 via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02250)). Note the Cd minimum sits almost exactly at our v=2 m/s point.
- Spinning TT ball, exactly our regime: Ito & **Kamijima** (not "Ueshima" — the paper romanizes 上島慶 as "Kei Kamijima"; Niigata Inst. Tech.), "Estimation of Aerodynamic Properties of a Spinning Table Tennis Ball Using CFD Analysis," Trans. JSST 17(1):25–31, 2025, Smagorinsky–Lilly LES, ANSYS Fluent, Vx 2.5–20 m/s, 15–90 rps, Re 6639.5–53115.8, SP 0.094–4.524 ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tjsst/17/1/17_25/_article/-char/en); PDF Table 2, ito.txt lines ~145–160). **I verified the literature agent's Table 2 values verbatim**: CD @15 rps = 0.583/0.498/0.475/0.490/0.493/0.490 (2.5→20 m/s); @90 rps = 0.588/0.588/0.510/0.551/0.555/0.548; full-grid span 0.475–0.620. Paper text: CD increases as speed decreases or spin increases (ito.txt §3.2).
- Within 2–8 m/s only: 15-rps CD goes 0.583→~0.48 (interpolating 5→10 m/s), i.e. ~±10%; adding spin dependence widens to 0.498–0.620 at 2.5–5 m/s. Validation is only "qualitative agreement" with wind-tunnel/flight experiments (abstract), and paper §3.2 notes its CD runs ~0.07 above ref [5] flight data.

JUDGMENT: "Effectively constant Cd" is defensible at the **±10–15% level** in 2–8 m/s — no drag-crisis cliff, no order-unity drift — so constant k_d is the right model class; but both agents' finer point stands: a single k_d hides a real ~10–20%, spin-coupled variation, and TT-specific aero data below Re 3e4 (v ≲ 11 m/s) is **CFD-only** (Miyazaki's flight experiments start at Re 3.0e4; the ISJOS/wind-tunnel data likewise ≥3e4). The failure agent's blanket "constant Cd is safe" is slightly too strong; the literature agent's "±15%" is the accurate statement.

## Sub-claim 2: SR values and Magnus saturation — arithmetic CONFIRMED; saturation CONFIRMED as documented, but for TT balls at SP>1 it is CFD-only

FACTS
- SR = ωR/v recomputed: 10 rev/s → 0.628/0.419/0.251/0.157 at 2/3/5/8 m/s; 30 rev/s → 1.885/**1.257**/0.754/0.471; 60 rev/s → 3.770/2.513/1.508/0.942. All quoted values in both agents (1.26, 1.88, 0.47, 2.51, pro 0.60) check out. Low-speed play at SR≈0.5–2.5(–3.8) is confirmed arithmetic. Ito Table 1 (ito.txt) uses identical SP values (e.g., 2.5 m/s/30 rps = 1.508). ✓
- Saturation, TT-specific: **Ito Table 3 verified verbatim** — CL at 2.5 m/s: 0.373/0.310/0.368/0.411/0.398/0.418 for SP 0.754→4.524 (plateau ~0.31–0.42 while SP grows 6×); at 5 m/s: 0.273–0.394. **Table 4 (CM) verified verbatim** — at 2.5 m/s CM falls 0.185→0.035 (15→90 rps, 5.3×); at 15 rps CM rises 0.185→0.338 (2.5→15 m/s). Paper text: CD and CL follow "increase → decrease → increase" vs SP; CM decreases with decreasing speed or increasing spin (ito.txt §3.2).
- **I verified the CM normalization claim numerically**: the identity CL = (8/3)·SP·CM holds across the grid (2.5 m/s/15 rps: (8/3)(0.754)(0.185)=0.372 vs CL 0.373; 5 m/s/15 rps: (8/3)(0.377)(0.271)=0.272 vs 0.273). So CM is defined via F = ρV_ball·CM·(ω×v) — genuinely the k_m-like ω×v coefficient. Conversion: k_m = ρV_ball·CM/m = 0.01494·CM; **v0's k_m=0.0042 ↔ CM=0.281**, and the failure agent's implied C_L = 0.752·SR slope is exact (0.750 recomputed). Constant k_m vs Table 4: overpredicts Magnus **3.3× at SP=1.5, 4.6× at SP=2.26, 8× at SP=4.5** — the failure agent's "2–4×" is conservative for SR>2.5.
- Saturation, generic spheres: experiments at SR 0–0.5 show CL rising rapidly to ~0.3 then holding ([Kim et al./TSFP8](https://torroja.dmt.upm.es/congresos/TSFP_8_2013/papers/AER2D.pdf); [Monash IJHFF 2024](https://www.flair.monash.edu.au/publications/pdfs/SaHoTh_IJHFF_2024.pdf) studies SR to 1.7 with non-monotonic CL). Non-monotonicity/saturation of sphere CL is standard.
- Lift crisis: Miyazaki et al., EJP 38 024001 — measured range **3.0e4<Re<9.0e4, 0<SP<1**; deep CL valley near SP=0.5, lift nearly vanishing at Re=9.0e4 for 0.48<SP<0.5 ([IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6404/aa51ea)). Confirms lit agent; also confirms this is a high-Re phenomenon outside the 2–8 m/s band. Ito's Table 3 has **no negative CL** anywhere in our regime ✓.
- Inverse Magnus: [IntechOpen ch. 83844](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/83844) states it "has been experimentally confirmed" for TT balls at low spin but "results only in deviations of some millimeters."

JUDGMENT: Saturation is a real, documented effect — but stratify the support: (a) sphere-generic CL saturation at SR ≳ 0.3–0.5: well-supported experimentally; (b) TT ball at SP up to 4.5 in the 2–8 m/s band: supported by exactly **one 2025 LES study** whose validation against experiment is qualitative and only overlaps experiments at Re≥2.65e4, SP≤1. The direction and rough magnitude (CL plateau ≈0.4, CM collapse) are trustworthy; the precise Table 3/4 numbers should be treated as ±20–30% priors, to be replaced by the team's mocap fits. The failure agent's assumed plateau 0.55 is *above* the CFD's 0.40 — their 6–41 cm saturation-gap estimates are therefore conservative in the plateau sense. One more CFD-supported point both agents made correctly: Ito Table 5 (verified: Cm from 1.11e-2 at 2.5 m/s/15 rps to 2.23e-1 at 2.5 m/s/90 rps) says spin decay is strongest exactly at low speed/high spin, where v0 holds ω constant in flight.

## Sub-claim 3: velocity dependence of table restitution at 1–4 m/s — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED; the 1–4 m/s slope itself is UNVERIFIABLE from open sources

FACTS
- ITTF spec arithmetic: 24–26 cm rebound from 30.5 cm ⇒ e = √(h2/h1) = **0.887–0.923 at v_n = √(2g·0.305) = 2.446 m/s**, on a standard steel block — recomputed ✓ and corroborated ("CoR between 0.89 and 0.92 for a 0.3 m free fall on a standard steel block", via [ISJOS search results](https://www.isjos.org/pdfs/ISJOS_v15_p2.pdf) context).
- Rémond/Dolique/Géminard, PRE 106 014207 (2022), normal impact on glass: "the restitution coefficient **continuously decreases** when the impact velocity is increased and a clear change in regime appears when the ball shell buckles"; buckling at crushing ≈(1.3±0.2) mm ≈ 2× shell thickness (500±20 µm) ([Semantic Scholar/search abstract](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4dfd4b6fa99570bc9d211bcc02126d7417cb432); full text 403-blocked at APS and HAL). So even pre-buckling the COR is *not* flat — but no open-access slope number.
- IntechOpen: buckling "for impact speeds above about 5 m/s"; vibrations "reduce the CoR from 0.9 to 0.8 or even below"; celluloid CoR "drops more quickly for initial velocities larger than 20 km/h" (5.6 m/s); contact 0.7 ms ([IntechOpen](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/83844)). Confirms lit agent's quotes.
- IOP drop test (ICMER2011, [IOP 36:012038](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1757-899X/36/1/012038/pdf), drop.txt Tables 3–4): TT ball on **steel**, drops 1.3→1.7 m (v1 5.05→5.77 m/s): COR 0.809→0.779 (local slope ≈ **−0.04/m/s**); on **wood** 0.786→0.743 (≈ −0.06/m/s); wood lowers TT COR ~4.3% vs steel. Cross-lab, cross-ball comparison ITTF(0.905 @2.45, steel) vs this (0.809 @5.05, steel) implies average ≈ **−0.037/m/s over 2.45→5.05 m/s** — but 5+ m/s is at/inside buckling onset, so much of that drop may be concentrated near 5 m/s.
- ISJOS v15 p2 (isjos.txt): **COR = −0.019u + 0.87** over 1.4–20.2 m/s — but this is ball on **paddle rubber** (RITC 729 backhand), not table; Kawazoe & Suzuki slope −0.012/m/s on rubber. This *strengthens* the racket-rubber velocity-dependence claim (consistent with the lit agent's 2604.11349 slopes 0.017–0.021/m/s) rather than the table claim.

JUDGMENT: The literature agent's framing is essentially right and the two survey statements are compatible: strong velocity dependence is **well documented above ~5 m/s** (buckling) and on rubber at all speeds; within **1–4 m/s on a rigid surface no published slope exists in the open sources I could reach** — PRE 2022 says "continuously decreases" (implying nonzero pre-buckling slope) while the 2604.11349 survey line says "depends only slightly." Bracketing from ITTF@2.45 and the drop test@5.05, the pre-buckling slope is bounded by roughly 0 to −0.04/m/s; the failure agent's hypothesized −0.01/m/s is comfortably inside this bracket, and their point that a v_n-slope hidden inside the 101-bounce median (narrow v_n 2.5–3.6 band) could eat the entire ±0.05 e-sensitivity is sound. Settling experiment: exactly what's already proposed — scatter e = −v_n⁺/v_n⁻ vs v_n⁻ from the venue mocap over v_n 1–5 m/s on the *actual table top* (also settles the steel-vs-wood-composite offset; note v0's e=0.908 is at the high end of even the steel ITTF band, and wood-like surfaces measured lower at 5 m/s).

## Corrections and thin-support flags (summary)

1. **CORRECTED (citation)**: CFD paper authors are Ito & **Kamijima** (2025), not "Ito & Ueshima"; all its quoted Table 2/3/4/5 numbers verified verbatim from the PDF.
2. **CONFIRMED**: Re and SR arithmetic everywhere; Miyazaki lift-crisis parameters (SP≈0.5 valley, Re 9e4, measured only for Re≥3e4/SP≤1); ITTF e-band 0.887–0.923 @2.45 m/s; k_m↔CM equivalence (CL=(8/3)·SP·CM identity holds; v0 k_m=0.0042 ↔ CM=0.281, at the top of Ito's CM range — constant k_m overpredicts by 3–8× at SR 1.5–4.5).
3. **THIN**: (a) all TT aero data in 2–8 m/s (Re<2.65e4) is one LES study, qualitatively validated; (b) rigid-surface COR slope specifically at 1–4 m/s — no open quantitative source (PRE full texts 403-blocked); bounded 0 to −0.04/m/s; (c) "Cd effectively constant" holds only to ±10–15% and the low-speed CD rise is spin-coupled, not a pure Re effect.
4. **Unverifiable here**: failure agent's simulated cm-level sensitivity grid (would require rerunning their integrator; their closed-form cross-checks — k_d fraction 1−e^(−k_d·L), Magnus/g hyperbola ω·v=2336, terminal v=8.96 m/s — all recompute correctly).
